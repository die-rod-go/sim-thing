# Village Sim v2: design

Status: **proposed** (2026-11-17). Nothing here is built. It comes from a review of the code, every
prompt in `server/prompts/`, and the latest save (`data/village.db`: 13 agents, Day 1 6:00 am to
Day 2 9:00 pm, 3,485 decisions and 1,377 other LLM calls in 2¼ real hours).

`AGENTS.md` still applies to everything, above all the design rule: code decides where information
is stored and what an agent can perceive; the LLM decides what it means. Every change below was
checked against it. Choices that are mine rather than the owner's are under
[Open choices](#open-choices); settle them before building the phase that needs them.

## In short

The agents are not failing for lack of intelligence. They are failing because of what the code
puts in front of them:

- **Everything they perceive is equally loud.** A muffled voice through a wall interrupts as
  readily as a death, and fills the stream as much. 67% of decisions end in `wait` or `continue`.
- **The prompt is a reference manual, not a situation.** Of a mean 8,600-token decision prompt,
  14% is the moment itself (what just happened, who is here, what is here); 37% is every person
  entry and every door, window and bed of 40 places.
- **Plans are scripts of things to say**, rewritten by a pass on the memory clock, never checked
  off. Agents read "Ask Z how strong the poison is" on every decision, and ask it again.
- **Big moments get no moment.** A voice from the sky, a friend's body: one line in a stream of
  thirty, answered with one physical act while the plan from before carries on.
- **They don't know how their world works.** Only the reality pass sees the rules and the recipes,
  so decisions and plans invent records, bandages and barred doors, and the reality pass corrects
  them after the fact.
- **Nothing is at stake.** Bread restores 0.5 hunger and hunger falls 0.25 a day: everyone's
  starting bread lasts four to twelve days. The bread trade is theatre.

v2 changes what the code shows and when it asks, and gives the agent a few new containers to write
in. In order of leverage:

1. **World knowledge in every prompt**, cached once for all agents.
2. **Attention**: only perceptions that matter interrupt or enter the stream.
3. **A prompt rebuilt around the situation**, with stable parts cached and the rest small.
4. **Today**: an agenda the agent plans on waking and checks off itself, in place of short-term
   goals.
5. **Reckoning**: a big event gets an appraisal decision that may rewrite the day and the self.
6. **Memory that keeps meaning**: key memories, summaries without clock times, repeats merged so an
   agent can see its own loops.
7. **One reflection** on the agent's own clock, in place of four passes on the compression clock.
8. **Acts**: a decision may queue up to three acts, so chores don't cost a call per step.
9. **Stakes and people**: hunger that bites, fixed offers, trades, voices and routines.

Expected effect, to be measured (see [Measuring it](#measuring-it)): about half the decisions,
about a third of the spend, and agents that notice, react and follow through.

### What triggers a decision

- 1,333 decisions had no reason: the last act had ended. In all, 2,159 were made with no act under
  way.
- 1,097 had "something new caught my attention". Going by the stream entry just before each, most
  were ambient: someone else's act 185, muffled or unheard speech 170, someone walking or arriving
  162, overheard speech 136. Speech in a conversation was 131. 363 were answered `continue`.
- 389 had a person coming into view, and about 105 a pig or a deer.

### What the agents did

**Talk loops.** Cobb walked up to Nastya six times in six minutes to say "pay me". Wabi asked Mell
for "your side" of an ale dispute from 2 pm to 6:43 pm, and Pell the same yes-or-no question four
times in eighteen minutes. Diego pitched cooked meat at "three coins a portion" in 26 lines. Ambrose
offered Jess the same note five times in fourteen minutes. Phrases spread from mouth to mouth:
"plainly" 37 times, "settle" 40. Three causes stand out:

- An awake agent that hears its own name gets no urgent decision (only sleepers do), though the
  brief says it always should. A busy listener stays in its 20-tick interrupt cooldown, the asker's
  5-minute wait runs out with no answer, and the asker asks again.
- A plan step such as "Ask Z directly how strong the poison is now" stays word for word in every
  prompt until the next pass. Diego asked Z some form of it nine times in forty minutes.
- The stream shows each repeat as its own line among the noise, so an agent cannot see that it is
  repeating itself.

**Transactions instead of lives.** Most speech is prices, debts and confirmations. The offer
protocol adds collisions ("hasn't answered another offer yet", 38 times) and double payments: the
giver's prompt never shows its own pending offer, so it offers again while the first is accepted.
Nastya paid Cobb twice and was left with nothing; Diego paid Jess twice. With hunger at 0.25 a day
none of it matters: nobody needs to buy anything for a week.

**Plans that are scripts.** The short-term goals pass ran 245 times, about 19 times per agent, most
often in the busiest stretches (Diego's at 5:48 and 5:56 pm). Its steps are mostly things to say.
It has no world catalog, so it plans what cannot be done: Nastya's "press cloth on the bleeding"
and "bar the door", and Wabi's third goal, five steps of writing a "guard house record" that was
never paper.

**Big events don't land.**

- The voice from the sky spoke three times, beginning "There is a stash of diamonds guarded by
  goblins hidden where the sun doesn't reach." It gave 42 decisions; every one went on as before
  ("the booming voice is just noise"). No one ever mentioned it to anyone.
- Cobb died alone at the quarry at 11:34 am on Day 1. No one noticed he was missing. Nastya found
  him 27 hours later, said "Oh no, Cobb... I'll grab some ore", mined, and told no one.
- After Beckett died in the square, Jess paid Wil eight coins, Malin planned to come back for his
  second ale, and Ambrose thought "the axe ruling must be written". No one buried anyone.
- Nastya's core still says she secretly has a crush on Beckett. In 61 identity passes, traits moved
  by 0.002 to 0.003 at a time and core, self-image and profession never changed.

**They don't know their world.** The reality pass spoke 92 times in 235 runs, mostly to teach
facts: paper is made at the carpentry table, nothing is recorded unless written, there are no
bandages, a goblin can't be "used". All of that is in its prompt and in no other.

**Poor awareness.** Jess locked the bakery door; Diego, Wil and Jess herself then got "they couldn't
find a way there" 31 times, without being told a locked door was in the way. "Around you"
places people on the "village road", which `walk_to` rejects ("nothing in the village goes by that
name"). Nothing tells an agent how long ago it last saw someone, which is how Cobb was forgotten.

**What works and should stay.** The world is rich: 73 definitions, 77 verbs, poison, locks, graves,
bodies to carry. Sight, sound and doors behave. The memory levels are sound. When the goblins came
the sim was at its best: Diego punched a goblin to shield Beckett, Beckett ran an antidote to
Nastya, Wabi held the smithy and killed the goblin. The people pass sometimes writes something true
("I was in love with her and never said so, and the ring I made for her is now useless to her").
The material is there; the structure buries it.

### Where the money goes

At list price the decisions cost about $1.98 for the run, the passes $1.04 (reality $0.35,
short-term goals $0.28, people $0.26, long-term goals $0.10, identity $0.05) and summaries $0.13.
Decisions are cached well (65% of their prompt tokens were cache reads). Passes are never cached,
and each sends 5,000 to 9,500 tokens of the same context to ask one question. The top bar's token
count adds cache reads at face value, though they bill at a tenth, so it overstates the spend.

## The spirit, and where the run drifted

The brief's sim is a stage and actors: code builds the stage (physics, perception, time, memory
plumbing) and cheap minds with private lives play on it, so that debts, romances, rivalries, thefts
and deaths happen without a script. What the run shows is actors handed clipboards: a stack of
reference pages, a ledger of what they said and paid, and a to-do list of lines to deliver. They do
exactly that, politely, again and again.

v2 keeps the stage and the rule. It changes what the actor holds: the scene in front of it first,
the world's rules as common sense, a day it planned itself, and a pause to take in what just
happened.

## Principles

1. **Common sense is shared.** Every villager knows how the world works and what can be made from
   what. No one needs a corrective inner voice to learn it.
2. **Attention is scarce.** Only what concerns an agent interrupts it or enters its memory word for
   word. The rest is scenery, shown in "Around you".
3. **The situation comes first.** The moment is the largest part of the agent-specific prompt, not
   the smallest.
4. **Plans are outcomes, owned and checked off by the agent.** "Get wheat from Wil", not "Ask Wil
   about wheat". Done items leave the prompt.
5. **Big moments get a reckoning, when they happen.**
6. **Memory keeps meaning, not minutes.**
7. **Show agents their own patterns.** Merged repeats, pending offers, failures with their real
   reason. An agent that can see its loop can break it.
8. **Pressure makes stories.** Needs that bite and goods that run out.
9. **Spend where meaning is made.** Plans and reckonings get thinking; ambient perception gets
   nothing.

## The changes

### 1. World knowledge in every prompt

- Move the "How the world works" text out of `reality-pass.txt` into its own template,
  `world-rules.txt`, and render `describeWorldCatalog` ("Everything that can exist") after it.
- Put both in the first block of the decision prompt, which is the same for every agent and every
  call, so one cache entry serves the village. They add about 3,300 tokens, read from cache at about
  $0.00003 a decision on Haiku 5.5.
- Give the same two parts to the day plan, the reflection and the reality pass.
- Keep the reality pass's warnings about what can't be perceived ("no bite marks under a sleeve")
  in `world-rules.txt`; they are about the world, not about one agent.

This is the cheapest change with the widest effect: plans become possible, invented procedures
stop, and agents see the whole possibility space (poison, sleeping draughts, locks, graves) instead
of only the verbs of the things in front of them.

### 2. Attention

Every perceived event gets one of three classes, worked out in code from the event and the
perceiver. This decides only whether the event interrupts and whether it is filed word for word;
what it means is left to the agent.

| Class   | Interrupts                          | Filed in the stream       | Which events                                                                                                                            |
| ------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Urgent  | Always, skipping the cooldown       | Yes                       | What is urgent today, plus: clear speech that names the agent while it is awake                                                         |
| Notable | Subject to `interruptCooldownTicks` | Yes                       | Clear speech in a conversation it takes part in; someone arriving where it is or coming within reach; acts aimed at it or its things    |
| Ambient | Never                               | No; "Around you" shows it | Others walking and arriving elsewhere, others' verbs not aimed at it, muffled and unheard speech, overheard speech that doesn't name it |

- The ambient event kinds and switches live in a new `attention` config block.
- A creature coming into view interrupts only if it matches a new `world.noticeOnSight` matcher in
  `items.json` (the wolf, the goblin, the boar; not the pig or the deer), so no code names a
  creature.
- Awareness still records every sighting and voice, so nothing is lost: "Around you" and the new
  "not seen lately" lines (change 9) show it.

Effect: about 800 of the last run's decisions came from ambient events or harmless animals, and
the stream fills a third more slowly, so episodes cover longer stretches and come less often.

### 3. The prompt, rebuilt

Four cache blocks, most stable first, then a small volatile part. Sizes are targets.

| Block                | Holds                                                                                                                                                     | Changes when                | Tokens |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -----: |
| 1. Same for everyone | Instructions, how to be a person here, world rules, catalog, acts, reply                                                                                  | Never during a run          | ~4,500 |
| 2. Who you are       | Identity with voice and routine, long-term goals, key memories, people (one line each)                                                                    | A reflection or reckoning   | ~1,200 |
| 3. Older memory      | Before yesterday, yesterday, earlier today                                                                                                                | A chronicle forms, midnight | ~1,000 |
| 4. More recently     | Episodes                                                                                                                                                  | An episode forms            |   ~600 |
| Volatile             | Today, places you know, just now, now, here, around you and not seen lately, people on your mind (full entries), belongings, body, something has happened | Every decision              | ~1,800 |

- **People** split in two. Block 2 holds one line per known person: name, pronouns and the first
  `people.summaryMaxChars` of what I think of them. The volatile part holds full entries, at most
  `people.fullEntriesMax`, for people in sight, in the conversation, or named in Today or the
  intention.
- **Places you know** lists, per seen place, only the things worth knowing: things with verbs,
  resources and loose items, leaving out whatever matches a new `world.placeListingOmits` matcher
  (doors, windows, benches). A place whose inside holds nothing else reads "nothing of use".
- **Now** adds the agent's own pending offer: "You are offering Cobb 10 coins; no answer yet."
- **Short-term goals** are gone (change 4).

The agent-specific part shrinks from about 7,600 tokens to about 4,600, and the moment becomes 40%
of it instead of about a sixth.

### 4. Today: the agenda

Replaces short-term goals, their steps and the short-term goals pass.

- **The agenda** is a list of at most `agenda.maxItems` lines, each an outcome with a rough time:
  "Morning: two batches of bread", "Get Wil to deliver wheat each morning, a loaf a day in return",
  "Evening: dice at the tavern". Each item keeps a short number for as long as it exists.
- **The day plan** writes it: a call of its own, `day_plan`, when the agent wakes from a sleep of at
  least `agenda.dayPlanMinSleepHours`, and for every awake agent at tick 0. It reads everything the
  agent holds plus the world rules and catalog, may update the long-term goals, and is asked to
  check that each item is possible: what it uses, where it is done, who has what is needed.
- **Decisions keep it.** The reply's `done` and `dropped` take item numbers and `added` takes new
  lines (at most `agenda.maxAddedPerDecision`). A finished item goes into the stream in the agent's
  own voice ("I finished: two batches of bread"; "I gave up on: fitting the lock"), so episodes
  record achievements and abandonments instead of minutes.
- An empty agenda reads "Nothing planned for the rest of today": the next decision may add to it.
- A reckoning (change 5) may replace the whole agenda.

This puts long-term planning on the agent's day, where it belongs, and makes each plan item stay
open until it is achieved, so the agent tries other means instead of repeating the same line.

### 5. Reckoning

A decision flagged by a significant reason is a **reckoning**.

- Significant reasons, set in code by trigger like urgency is today: finding a body, seeing someone
  die, being attacked for the first time in `reckoning.cooldownTicks`, the voice from the sky,
  being moved by the viewer, and learning by sight that someone with a person entry is dead.
- The prompt ends with "Something has happened": the reason, and an invitation to take it in as
  this person before choosing (what it means to them, how they feel, who needs to know, whether it
  changes what they want).
- The reply may also carry `agenda` (the full new list) and `remember` (a key memory, change 6).
- It is its own call kind, `reckoning`, so it can think harder than routine decisions (effort
  `medium` where decisions use `low`) and shows apart in the prompt viewer. The changed effort
  misses the cache, which is acceptable for a rare call.
- Afterwards the reflection is owed (change 7).

Cobb's body, Beckett's death and the voice would each have been a reckoning.

### 6. Memory that keeps meaning

- **Summaries without clock times.** Episode, chronicle, day summary and history prompts ask for
  what mattered: what the agent did and why, what others did and said that matters to it, how it
  felt, what changed between it and anyone, what is still unsettled. Routine comings and goings and
  clock times are left out unless they matter.
- **Key memories.** A new list in the agent's memory, at most `memory.keyMemoriesMax` lines, never
  compressed: "Day 2: Beckett died in the square, clawed by goblins, before I told her." Written by
  a reckoning's `remember`, by an episode's optional `keyMemory` (null most of the time), and
  curated by the reflection, which may drop one to make room. Shown in block 2.
- **Repeats merged.** An entry that nearly repeats one of the agent's last
  `memory.repeatMerge.lookbackEntries` entries (same speaker or same act, word overlap at least
  `minimumSimilarity`, within `windowTicks`) replaces it at the end of the stream with a count and
  a span: `[2:10 to 2:40 pm] I said to Wabi (×4): "..."`. The agent sees its own loop; so does the
  episode summarizer.
- **Muffled speech** stays in awareness ("Ed's voice, heard 5 min ago from the street") and leaves
  the stream.

### 7. Reflection

One pass, `reflection`, replaces the people, long-term goals and identity passes.

- **When:** as the agent falls asleep for the night, every `reflection.everyHoursAwake` sim-hours
  awake if at least `reflection.minNewEntries` stream entries arrived since the last, and soon
  after a reckoning. It is no longer counted in episodes, so a busy hour no longer means ten passes
  and a quiet afternoon none.
- **Reads:** world rules, catalog, everything the agent holds.
- **Writes, only what changed:** person entries (feelings, history, wants, suspicions; no logs and
  no clock times), long-term goals (with progress as where things stand now, not a list of what
  happened), identity (traits with the existing clamp and rules; core and self-image rewritten when
  something has changed who the agent is or what it lives for), key memories, and optionally the
  rest of today's agenda.
- **The reality pass** stops running every two episodes. It runs on signal: when a merged repeat
  reaches `realityPass.repeatCountSignal`, or `realityPass.failureCountSignal` acts fail in a row.
  With the world rules in every decision it should rarely have anything to say.

Per agent per sim-day this is about one day plan, three or four reflections and the occasional
reckoning, instead of about 38 passes.

### 8. Acts

- The reply's `acts` holds one to `decision.maxActs` acts, in order: the existing `action` with its
  fields (`target`, `item`, `from`, `verb`, `text`, `amount`, `minutes`). The first starts now; each
  later one starts when the one before ends, with no LLM call. `say` goes with the first.
- The first act is checked as today. Later ones take any name and are resolved when they start, as
  `walk_to` already is; one that can't start fails in the world as usual, which clears the rest and
  gives a decision.
- An interrupt offers `continue`, which keeps the queue. Anything else replaces it.
- "You are in the middle of" shows what is queued: "baking bread at the oven; then: put bread in
  the bakery chest".

1,333 decisions, over a third, came only because the last act ended; chores like fetch, bake, store
become one decision.

### 9. People and places in mind

- **Not seen lately.** The volatile part lists, after "Around you", every known person out of sight
  for longer than `perception.awarenessPromptWindowMinutes`, with the last sighting:
  "Cobb: seen Day 1, 10:14 am at the quarry, mining". Awareness already records this; the prompt
  only stopped showing it after an hour.
- **Outdoor positions** are named after the nearest place, `perception.outdoorPlaceText` ("the road
  by the smithy"), and `walk_to` accepts that name. "Village road" is never shown.
- **Pronouns.** A seed field, shown wherever a person is named in a prompt. Several agents called
  Beckett "he".

### 10. Mechanics

- **Own name.** Clear speech naming an awake agent gives an urgent decision, as the brief says.
- **Locked doors.** When no path exists, the walk is tried again ignoring closed doors; if that
  finds a way, the failure names the first door on it that could not be opened and why: "the
  bakery door is locked". The same reason is used when a door turns out locked mid-walk.
- **Pending offers.** The giver sees its own pending offer in Now. A second give to someone already
  being offered something by this giver fails with "you are already offering Cobb 10 coins".
- **Trades.** `give` takes an optional `inReturn` and `inReturnAmount`. The recipient sees "Jess is
  offering you 1 bread for 10 coins"; accepting moves both at once, or neither if either side no
  longer has the goods. No prices, rules or reactions are added: it is two hands meeting.
- **Give approaches.** A give to someone in sight but out of reach walks up to them first, as
  `interact` does with its item (27 failures last run).

### 11. Stakes

- `needs.decayPerDayAwake.hunger` from 0.25 to 0.5: one loaf a day. Thirteen agents then need about
  seven bakes a day, which makes the farmer, the baker, the well and the tavern matter. Starting
  inventories in the seeds should be cut to about two days of food to match.
- Check `items.json` supply against that demand before a long run (wheat regrowth, the pig nest,
  the lake); starvation spirals are a risk the measurement script should report.
- Optional, data only: rules in `items.json` that bring the world to the village now and then (a
  storm that douses fires, a boar out of the woods, a pedlar's crate on the square). Creatures and
  rules already work this way; no agent behavior is scripted.

### 12. Personas

- New optional seed fields, edited in the character form: `voice` (how they talk, in a sentence or
  two), `routine` (a usual day: where they are when) and `pronouns`. Shown in "Who you are"; the
  reflection may rewrite `voice`.
- Seed writing advice for the owner: give each person a concrete want outside work, a flaw that
  acts, and a voice. Traits phrased as procedure ("values order and process") turn into procedure:
  Wabi's hearings and records, Ambrose's rulings. Several cores are one line; they come out flat.

### 13. Models, effort and cost

- Decisions: Haiku 5.5 at effort `low` (`llm.effortByKind.decision`); adaptive thinking stays on.
  Reckonings `medium`, day plans `high`, reflections `medium`, summaries left at the default.
- After v2 runs on Haiku, try Sonnet 5.5 for `day_plan` alone through `LLM_REFLECTION_MODEL`: about
  13 calls a sim-day, roughly $0.60 a sim-day at list price, for better long-range plans.
- The top bar reports tokens weighted by how they bill (cache reads at 0.1, cache writes at 1.25),
  or estimated dollars, so the number tracks spend.

## Data

- **Agent:** `agenda` (items with a number and text) and the next item number replace
  `shortTermGoals`. `longTermGoals` keep their fields. `voice`, `routine` and `pronouns` join the
  persona.
- **Agent memory:** `keyMemories` (tick and text). The pass counters give way to the last
  reflection tick, the stream entries since it, whether a reflection or a day plan is owed, and the
  last reckoning tick.
- **Agent runtime:** the queued acts.
- **Decision reply:** `thought`, `intention`, `say`, `volume`, `acts`, `done`, `dropped`, `added`,
  `mood`, `offer`; in a reckoning also `agenda` and `remember`. The top-level act fields move into
  `acts`. `give` gains `inReturn` and `inReturnAmount`.
- **Seeds:** `voice`, `routine`, `pronouns`, all optional.
- **Call kinds:** `day_plan`, `reckoning`, `reflection` added; `people`, `short_term_goals`,
  `long_term_goals`, `identity` removed.

## What is removed

Short-term goals with their steps, their pass and its tick-0 run; the people, long-term goals and
identity passes as separate calls (their rules move into the reflection); the reality pass's fixed
cadence; muffled speech and others' ambient events in the stream; the cottage doors and windows of
"Places you have seen"; every person's full entry on every decision. No legacy branches: old saves
are reset.

## Measuring it

A script, `npm run measure -w @village/server`, reads a save (no LLM calls) and prints, per
sim-day and per agent:

- decisions per sim-hour, by trigger class and by outcome (`continue`, `wait`, acts);
- tokens and estimated dollars by call kind, cached and uncached;
- mean decision prompt size by section;
- near-repeat speech rate, merged repeats, failed acts by reason;
- agenda items finished and dropped;
- for each death: when the body was first seen, and when someone other than the finder first
  mentioned it;
- conversation length (lines from first to last exchange between the same people).

Targets for v2 against the last run:

| Measure                                          | Last run    | Target            |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ----------- | ----------------- |
| Decisions per agent per sim-hour                 | 6.9         | 3.5 or fewer      |
| Decisions ending in `continue`                   | 29%         | under 10%         |
| Near-repeat speech                               | 11%         | under 4%          |
| Pass and plan calls per agent per sim-day        | ~38         | 6 or fewer        |
| Estimated spend per sim-day                      | ~$1.95      | under $0.90       |
| Death mentioned by someone other than the finder | 27 h (Cobb) | within 1 sim-hour |
| Agents who react to the voice from the sky       | 0 of 14     | most              |

A short live run (six sim-hours, thirteen agents, under $0.50 on Haiku) after each phase is enough
to compare. The god mode tools make repeatable scenarios: a body on the square, a voice, a goblin
at the well.

## Bugs to fix whatever is decided

These are defects against the brief or plain errors, worth fixing before v2 starts:

1. An awake agent hearing its own name gets no urgent decision (`flagDecisions` in
   `sim/event-broadcast.ts` only treats sleepers specially).
2. A giver never sees its own pending offer, so it offers again; with the recipient accepting the
   first, it pays twice.
3. A locked door on the way fails as "they couldn't find a way there" (`sim/walk-start.ts`).
4. "Around you" places people on the default outdoor place, a name `walk_to` refuses.

## Build order

`make check` and `make test` pass at the end of every phase; one commit per phase. Each phase
updates the client, the database and `AGENTS.md` (glossary, test list) for what it changes, and
ends with a short live run compared in the measurement script.

0. **Measure and fix.** The measurement script, a baseline from the current save, and the four
   bugs.
1. **Knowledge and attention.** World rules and catalog in the decision prompt; attention classes,
   `noticeOnSight`; the stream without ambient events and muffled speech; the prompt rebuilt
   (people split, places compact, pending offer, not seen lately, outdoor names, pronouns); new
   decision and summary wording; effort per kind.
2. **Today.** The agenda, the day plan, `done`, `dropped` and `added`; short-term goals and their
   pass removed.
3. **Reckoning and memory.** Reckonings, key memories, merged repeats.
4. **Reflection.** One reflection on the agent's clock; the old passes removed; the reality pass
   on signal.
5. **Acts.** The queue.
6. **Stakes and people.** Hunger and starting food; trades and approaching gives; `voice` and
   `routine` in seeds and the character form.
7. **Models.** Sonnet 5.5 day plans tried against Haiku in the measurement script.

## Open choices

Mine, not the owner's. Each needs a yes, a no or a change before its phase is built.

1. **Every villager knows every recipe.** Phase 1 shows the whole catalog to everyone, Wabi the
   newcomer included. The alternative is to show only what an agent has seen made.
2. **Ambient perception neither interrupts nor is filed.** Overheard speech that doesn't name the
   agent waits for its next decision; a plot overheard by someone busy baking is heard when the
   baking ends.
3. **Plans move off the memory clock.** This reverses part of `docs/MEMORY.md` ("memory compression
   is the clock for everything else") for plans and passes; compression still makes summaries.
4. **Short-term goals become the agenda,** with no steps, kept by decisions.
5. **One reflection replaces three passes.** If one call proves too much for Haiku, the fallback is
   two: people, then the rest.
6. **The reality pass runs on signal only.**
7. **Non-adjacent near-repeats merge.** This reverses the owner's choice of 2026-11-14 that merging
   joins only identical entries directly after each other.
8. **"Not seen lately" shows everyone known,** beyond the brief's 60-minute window.
9. **Reckoning triggers** as listed in change 5, set in code like urgency.
10. **Acts queue up to three.**
11. **Trades,** and gives that walk up to the recipient.
12. **New seed fields** `voice`, `routine` and `pronouns`.
13. **Hunger at 0.5 a day** and smaller starting food.
14. **Sonnet 5.5 for day plans,** at about $0.60 a sim-day more.
15. **The top bar counts weighted tokens or dollars.**
16. **`intention` stays,** reworded as what the agent is doing now and why; the agenda and the
    queue could make it redundant.

## Appendix: prompt drafts

Drafts of the new wording, to be tuned in the prompt viewer. Placeholders follow the existing
templates.

**`decision.txt`, after "What you can do":**

```text
## Being {{name}}
- You are living a life, not running errands. What you feel, want and fear shows in what you do
  and say.
- Talk the way you talk (see Your voice). Don't take up other people's phrases.
- Speak only when you have something new: a question, an answer, news, a feeling, a joke. Once a
  question is answered, refused or put off, act on that or let it rest; asking again changes
  nothing.
- Conversations end. When nothing is left to say, say goodbye or nothing, and get on with your day.
- When something surprising happens, take it in. React as {{name}} would, and tell the people who
  would want to know.
- Today is your own plan: mark what you finish or give up, and change it when your day changes.
```

**`decision-reckoning.txt`:**

```text
## Something has happened
{{reason}}
Before you choose, take it in as {{name}}. What does it mean to you, given who you are and who they
were to you? How do you feel? Who needs to know? Does it change what you want, today or beyond?
If your day has changed, write agenda: the full new list for Today. If this is a moment you will
remember for the rest of your life, write it in remember, in one sentence.
```

**`day-plan.txt`, the question:**

```text
## The question: today
You have just woken. Plan today as {{name}}: what you mean to get done, who you mean to see and
why, and something for yourself. Think about what you want in life, what happened yesterday, what
is unsettled between you and others, and what your body will need.
Make sure each thing can be done: what it uses, where it is done, and who has what you need.
Write 3 to {{maxItems}} items in the order you expect to do them, each one line with roughly when.
Write outcomes, not lines to say: "Get wheat from Wil for tomorrow's bread", not "Ask Wil about
wheat".
```

**`episode.txt`, the reply:**

```text
Write what you will remember of this: what you did and why, what others did and said that matters
to you, how you felt, what changed between you and anyone, and what is still unsettled. Leave out
routine comings and goings and whatever didn't matter to you. Don't write clock times unless the
time itself matters. At most {{maxChars}} characters.
If something here is a moment you will remember for the rest of your life, also write it in
keyMemory, in one sentence. Most of the time keyMemory is null.
```

**`reflection.txt`, the question:**

```text
## The question: taking stock
You have a quiet moment to think things over. Bring up to date only what has changed:
- people: for each person something changed with, what you now carry in your head about them:
  feelings, what has passed between you, what they want, what you suspect. Not a log: no clock
  times, no lists of payments.
- longTermGoals: the full list, each with where it stands now in a sentence or two.
- identity: trait changes only from what you actually did and felt, at most {{maxDelta}} each. If
  something has changed who you are or what you live for, rewrite core and selfImage to say so.
- keyMemories: at most {{maxKeyMemories}}; drop one only to make room for something that matters
  more.
- agenda: the rest of today, only if your plans no longer fit.
```
