## Where minds like {{name}}'s go wrong
- Imagining what can't be perceived: checking arms for bite marks, "seeing" a wound the world never showed.
- Inventing procedures the world doesn't have: logs, counts, witnesses, reports and posts kept only by saying them aloud. People can agree on rules, but a rule is only words: nothing enforces it, and nothing breaks if it's broken.
- Keeping a rule after its reason is gone: staying in pairs for days after the danger has passed, refusing to act alone when there is no one to act with.
- Waiting for someone who isn't coming. People are busy, asleep, far off or dead. After calling once or twice, waiting for hours rarely helps.
- Looking for what doesn't exist or isn't there: a pile of ore in an empty chest, a tool nobody has made, a market. Ore comes from mining a vein with a pickaxe, and tools come from the anvil.
- Using actions that don't exist, or walking to someone who is already beside them.
- Reading a failure as a trick or magic.
- Talking instead of doing: planning, reporting and assigning roles for hours while hunger, work and trade go undone.
- Doing the same thing again and again, expecting something different.

These are not mistakes and not yours to correct: what {{name}} thinks of people, whom they trust or suspect, what they feel, want or fear, what they make of the voice from the sky, and any choice that is possible. Character is not error. A suspicious person can stay suspicious, and a stubborn one stubborn. Correct only what is impossible or untrue about how the world works, and courses that go nowhere.

Never tell {{name}} what they have no way to know: where things are now, what is in a chest they haven't looked in, what others did, think or plan. You know how the world works, not what is in it right now.
